# REGISTER SEED — prose voice for the game

**Status: DRAFT for a builder.** Distilled from three source voices, all read in full text, not
from memory: Voltaire's *Candide* (1918 Modern Library English), Cervantes' *Don Quixote*
(Ormsby, 1885), and Kafka's *The Trial* / *Metamorphosis* (Wyllie). Provenance and one licensing
caveat: `PROVENANCE.md`.

This governs **quest text, item descriptions, NPC and institutional dialogue, and combat
narration**. It does not govern systems, UI labels, or tutorial copy.

The three voices agree on one thing, and that agreement *is* the register: **the narrator never
tells you how to feel about what just happened.** Voltaire reports a massacre as arithmetic.
Cervantes reports a delusion as fact and lets a shabby object sit next to a glorious name
without adjudicating. Kafka reports institutional cruelty in the courteous vocabulary of the
institution itself. Every law below is a way of not breaking that.

---

## THE LAWS

### LAW 1 — Report catastrophe in the register of a clerk

Losses are counts, prices, durations, and procedures. The prose does the sums and moves on.

- **DO:** give the damage as a number, a price, or a schedule — *"the mill snatches hold of a
  finger, they cut off the hand."*
- **DON'T:** attach an adverb or clause that tells the player it was terrible (*"horrifically,"
  *"in a scene of unimaginable suffering"*). The number is the horror.

### LAW 2 — Institutions explain thoroughly and settle nothing

An office is never rude and never refuses. It helps, at length, with perfect internal
consistency, and the help terminates in another step.

- **DO:** let an NPC give a complete, courteous, correct-sounding procedure whose outcome is a
  further procedure.
- **DON'T:** let an institution say *"I can't help you"* or stonewall. Obstruction that announces
  itself is the wrong flavour — the menace is that it is *cooperating*.

### LAW 3 — The name of a thing outranks the thing

Grand provenance and shabby matter occupy the same sentence, and nobody arbitrates.

- **DO:** give an item a sonorous name with a history, then describe its physical condition
  flatly and without comment (a barber's basin *is* Mambrino's helmet, and it is missing half).
- **DON'T:** wink. No "(actually just a bucket)", no narrator confirming which reading is true.
  The gap is the joke and explaining it kills it.

### LAW 4 — Doctrine survives its own refutation

A character states the governing principle, reality demolishes it, and they restate it
undamaged. They are not lying and not secretly aware.

- **DO:** have the doctrine reasserted immediately after the disproof, with fresh evidence
  offered in its favour.
- **DON'T:** let them acknowledge the contradiction, get a flicker of doubt, or deliver the
  doctrine "hollowly." Sincerity is what makes it land.

### LAW 5 — Courtesy is the medium of harm

Violence arrives wearing ceremony: music, procedure, scheduling, good manners.

- **DO:** dress harm in ritual and politeness — whipping *in cadence, while they were singing*.
- **DON'T:** use villain-voice, threats, sneering, or anyone who enjoys it. Nobody in this
  register relishes cruelty; they administer it.

### LAW 6 — Chain misfortunes without a reaction beat

Disasters connect with plain conjunctions. No paragraph break for grief, no pause to absorb.

- **DO:** run consequences together — *"and when we attempt to run away, they cut off the leg;
  both cases have happened to me."*
- **DON'T:** give a disaster a beat of silence, a stunned line of dialogue, or a scene break
  before the next one lands. The relentlessness is the comedy and the horror both.

### LAW 7 — The body is inventoried, not dramatized

Injury is a change in an object's condition. It persists, it is logged, it becomes furniture.

- **DO:** track damage as a durable state (an apple lodged in the back, still there a month
  later; a character down to one buttock who mentions it in passing).
- **DON'T:** reach for pain adjectives, gore texture, or agony. Nobody screams in this register.

### LAW 8 — Resolution is a small practical task

The ending is modest, concrete, and unheroic. No transcendence, no stated moral.

- **DO:** close on a manageable chore that quietly declines the whole preceding argument.
- **DON'T:** end with triumph, a lesson learned, or a line that tells the player what it meant.

## EXEMPLARS

Thirty-three passages, each verbatim from the source files and machine-checked against them.
Citations give work + chapter. *Quixote* chapter numbers restart in Part II, so the part is
always named.

### A. Wandering / quest voice

> after having composed, struck out, rejected, added to, unmade, and remade a multitude of names
> out of his memory and fancy, he decided upon calling him Rocinante, a name, to his thinking,
> lofty, sonorous, and significant of his condition as a hack before he became what he now was,
> the first and foremost of all the hacks in the world.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. I

> Fortune is arranging matters for us better than we could have shaped our desires ourselves,
> for look there, friend Sancho Panza, where thirty or more monstrous giants present themselves,
> all of whom I mean to engage in battle and slay, and with whose spoils we shall begin to make
> our fortunes
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. VIII

> "What giants?" said Sancho Panza. / "Those thou seest there," answered his master, "with the
> long arms, and some have them nearly two leagues long."
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. VIII

> that same sage Friston who carried off my study and books, has turned these giants into mills
> in order to rob me of the glory of vanquishing them, such is the enmity he bears me; but in
> the end his wicked arts will avail but little against my good sword.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. VIII *(Law 4 in its purest form — the defeat becomes evidence.)*

> Day was dawning when Don Quixote quitted the inn, so happy, so gay, so exhilarated at finding
> himself now dubbed a knight, that his joy was like to burst his horse-girths.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. IV

> We are at the end of all our troubles, and at the beginning of happiness.
— *Candide*, ch. XIX *(spoken immediately before the worst chapter of the book)*

> Someone must have been telling lies about Josef K., he knew he had done nothing wrong but, one
> morning, he was arrested.
— *The Trial*, ch. I *(the whole register in one sentence: grievance, innocence, and the flat
report of the catastrophe, all in the same clause weight)*

> "I know also," said Candide, "that we must cultivate our garden."
— *Candide*, ch. XXX *(Law 8 — the model for a final quest resolution)*

### B. Item / object voice

> "By God the basin is a good one, and worth a real of eight if it is worth a maravedis,"
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. XXI *(the appraisal register: an item priced by someone who can see
what it is)*

> Clearly the pagan to whose measure this famous head-piece was first forged must have had a
> very large head; but the worst of it is half of it is wanting.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. XXI *(the same object, described by someone who cannot — grand
provenance and a flat defect note, no arbitration. **This pair is the template for item
descriptions.**)*

> "It is a balsam," answered Don Quixote, "the receipt of which I have in my memory, with which
> one need have no fear of death, or dread dying of any wound
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. X

> thou hast nothing to do when in some battle thou seest they have cut me in half through the
> middle of the body—as is wont to happen frequently—but neatly and with great nicety, ere the
> blood congeal, to place that portion of the body which shall have fallen to the ground upon
> the other half which remains in the saddle, taking care to fit it on evenly and exactly.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. X *(consumable-item usage text: precise procedure, absurd premise,
delivered with the calm of a manual. Note "as is wont to happen frequently.")*

> Observe, that the nose has been formed to bear spectacles--thus we have spectacles. Legs are
> visibly designed for stockings--and we have stockings.
— *Candide*, ch. I *(objects as proof of cosmic design — a good voice for an in-world scholar
describing loot)*

> The mitre and _san-benito_ belonging to Candide were painted with reversed flames and with
> devils that had neither tails nor claws; but Pangloss's devils had claws and tails and the
> flames were upright.
— *Candide*, ch. VI *(equipment description as bureaucratic classification — the iconography is
graded, and the grading decides who burns)*

> An apple thrown without much force glanced against Gregor's back and slid off without doing
> any harm. Another one however, immediately following it, hit squarely and lodged in his back
— *Metamorphosis*, II

> No-one dared to remove the apple lodged in Gregor's flesh, so it remained there as a visible
> reminder of his injury. He had suffered it there for more than a month
— *Metamorphosis*, II *(Law 7 — a wound that becomes a persistent object. Model for a status
effect's flavour text.)*

### C. Institutional / shop voice

> It's up to you which one you choose. You can get either of them if I help you, but it'll take
> some effort of course, the difference between them is that apparent acquittal needs
> concentrated effort for a while and that deferment takes much less effort but it has to be
> sustained.
— *The Trial*, ch. VII *(**the canonical institutional-menu line.** Two options, clearly
explained, neither of which is acquittal.)*

> The text for an assertion of this sort was passed down to me from my father and it's quite
> unassailable.
— *The Trial*, ch. VII *(credentials offered as reassurance, guaranteeing nothing)*

> the first documents submitted are sometimes not even read by the court. They simply put them
> with the other documents and point out that, for the time being, questioning and observing the
> accused are much more important than anything written.
— *The Trial*, ch. VII

> If the applicant becomes insistent, then they add that before they come to any decision, as
> soon as all the material has been brought together, with due regard, of course, to all the
> documents, then these first documents to have been submitted will also be checked over. But
> unfortunately, even this is not usually true
— *The Trial*, ch. VII *(the "of course" and the "unfortunately" are doing all the work — the
speaker is on your side and it changes nothing)*

> it had been decided by the University of Coimbra, that the burning of a few people alive by a
> slow fire, and with great ceremony, is an infallible secret to hinder the earth from quaking.
— *Candide*, ch. VI *(institutional reasoning stated as settled fact, with a citation)*

> they had seized on a Biscayner, convicted of having married his godmother, and on two
> Portuguese, for rejecting the bacon which larded a chicken they were eating
— *Candide*, ch. VI *(charges listed with the precision of a docket; the disproportion is never
remarked on)*

> The landlord, to get him out of the inn, replied with no less rhetoric though with shorter
> words, and without calling upon him to pay the reckoning let him go with a Godspeed.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. III *(**the shopkeeper register** — a proprietor humouring a
customer and eating the loss to end the conversation)*

> "Something's fallen down in there", said the chief clerk in the room on the left.
— *Metamorphosis*, I *(an institution's representative registering a catastrophe as a minor
irregularity)*

> "Depressing view," said K. "The lie made into the rule of the world."
— *The Trial*, ch. IX *(how a character is permitted to object: briefly, wearily, with no effect)*

### D. Combat & violence narrated flatly

> There was never anything so gallant, so spruce, so brilliant, and so well disposed as the two
> armies. Trumpets, fifes, hautboys, drums, and cannon made music such as Hell itself had never
> heard.
— *Candide*, ch. III *(pageantry first — the flatness lands harder after the parade)*

> The cannons first of all laid flat about six thousand men on each side; the muskets swept away
> from this best of worlds nine or ten thousand ruffians who infested its surface. The bayonet
> was also a _sufficient reason_ for the death of several thousands. The whole might amount to
> thirty thousand souls.
— *Candide*, ch. III *(**the canonical flat-combat passage.** Casualties as a running total,
philosophical vocabulary applied to a bayonet, and the whole thing totted up in the last
sentence.)*

> Candide, who trembled like a philosopher, hid himself as well as he could during this heroic
> butchery.
— *Candide*, ch. III *("heroic butchery" — the only editorial in the passage, and it is two
words)*

> The regiment was composed of two thousand men; that composed for him four thousand strokes,
> which laid bare all his muscles and nerves, from the nape of his neck quite down to his rump.
— *Candide*, ch. II *(damage resolved as multiplication — the closest thing here to a combat-log
line)*

> As they were going to proceed to a third whipping, Candide, able to bear no more, begged as a
> favour that they would be so good as to shoot him. He obtained this favour
— *Candide*, ch. II *(Law 5 — the courtesy of the request and the grant)*

> Candide was whipped in cadence while they were singing; the Biscayner, and the two men who had
> refused to eat bacon, were burnt; and Pangloss was hanged, though that was not the custom. The
> same day the earth sustained a most violent concussion.
— *Candide*, ch. VI *(**Law 6's model sentence.** Four catastrophes, one semicolon-chain, and the
only note of surprise is a procedural irregularity — "though that was not the custom.")*

> as he drove his lance-point into the sail the wind whirled it round with such force that it
> shivered the lance to pieces, sweeping with it horse and rider, who went rolling over on the
> plain, in a sorry condition.
— *Don Quixote*, Part I, ch. VIII *(defeat narrated as physics; "in a sorry condition" is the
entire emotional content)*

> When we work at the sugar-canes, and the mill snatches hold of a finger, they cut off the hand;
> and when we attempt to run away, they cut off the leg; both cases have happened to me. This is
> the price at which you eat sugar in Europe.
— *Candide*, ch. XIX *(mutilation given as standing policy, in the passive institutional voice,
by the person it happened to — then priced)*

## ANTI-PATTERNS — what breaks this register

- **Epic-fantasy earnestness.** "Ancient evil," "the fate of the realm," prophecy, destiny,
  swelling stakes. This register has no stakes above the personal and inconvenient.
- **Snark, memes, fourth-wall winks.** The narrator being clever *about* the material. All three
  sources are funny because they are straight-faced; a knowing tone converts satire into comedy
  and loses the menace.
- **Grimdark.** Relished cruelty, gore texture, nihilist swagger, characters who enjoy being
  bad. Kafka's officials are *polite*; Voltaire's inquisitors are *procedurally satisfied*.
- **Explaining the joke.** Any line that confirms the basin is a basin, or notes that the
  bureaucracy is absurd. If the text adjudicates, Law 3 is dead.
- **Emotional stage directions.** "…he said sadly," "a chill ran through her," "the weight of it
  settled on him." The register reports; it does not coach.
- **Trauma-informed modern therapy-speak** in NPC mouths — processing, closure, healing,
  boundaries. Wrong century and wrong posture; these characters do not have interiors that work
  that way.
- **Competent, helpful institutions.** A shop or guild that efficiently solves the player's
  problem is off-register. It may *sell* efficiently; it may not *resolve*.
- **Escalating profanity or shock** as a substitute for flatness. The horror here is tonal, not
  lexical.

## OPEN QUESTIONS FOR RAY (design-axis, not builder-blocking)

1. **How much Quixote?** The Cervantes strand implies a protagonist whose *misreading* drives
   the quest text. That is a strong structural commitment, not just a prose flavour — it changes
   who the narrator is. Currently the laws support it but do not require it.
2. **Does the institutional voice get a single named body** (one Court/Ministry the player keeps
   returning to) or is it diffuse across every shop and guild? Kafka works because it is one
   unnameable thing; a diffuse version reads as world-flavour instead of dread.
3. **Is the player character sincere (Candide/Quixote) or bewildered-but-rational (Josef K.)?**
   The sources split here and the two produce very different quest text.
