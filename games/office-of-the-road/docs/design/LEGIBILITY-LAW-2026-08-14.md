# THE LEGIBILITY LAW — every number explains itself (Ray, 2026-08-14, binding)

Ray's words, the day the overhaul shipped: "it's mostly an administrative game - the UI
and UX NEEDS to be clear and understandable, like pick up and play, having numbers and
shit without any clear obvious explanation as to what they are in what is essentially an
administrative/idle game is counterproductive."

## The law

**Every number, token, and abbreviation rendered on any surface must be self-explanatory
at the point of reading, to a first-session player, with no manual and no memory.**

This is not a polish preference. OOR's whole premise is administration: the player IS the
desk. An administrative game whose own instruments read as cryptic paperwork is
self-defeating — and the register agrees, because a well-run office labels its ledgers.
Pick-up-and-play legibility is part of the game's fiction, not a concession to it.

## What satisfies the law

- A label at the point of reading: `atk 19`, `supplies 40`, `leg 3 of 5`.
- A plain word instead of a token: `encounters 0`, not `enc 0`.
- A universally-read format in an anchored context: `43/43` directly beside an hp bar
  reads as hp; the same `30/120` floating in a masthead reads as nothing.
- The shop detail-band pattern: the focused thing explains itself in a sentence.

## What violates it

- Single-letter fusions: `a19 d7 m4` (the founding violation).
- Bare `X/Y` tokens with no adjacent anchor: `30/120`.
- Sigils and at-tokens: `@30`, `(M)`, `71G` where `G` was never established on-surface.
- Any string a first-session player would have to ask about. When in doubt, it violates.

## Enforcement

1. **The full-surface number audit** (2026-08-14): every rendered numeric/token across
   all capture states, classified labeled-vs-cryptic; every cryptic token fixed or given
   its label. Repeat the audit at any milestone that adds a surface.
2. **Mechanical lint** for the known violation shapes (single-letter+digit fusions, bare
   `@N`, bare `X/Y` outside anchored contexts) in the text gate, so the founding
   violations cannot regress.
3. **Looker checklist item** on every art/UI round: "could a stranger say what every
   number on this frame means?"
4. Ray's eye remains the final gate; his read outranks any green lint.

Relation: this is OOR's local instance of the fleet's legibility-floor gate, tightened
because the genre is administrative. MATERIAL BREACH (the admin sibling) carries the
same law via its DIRECTIONS; any future admin/idle title inherits it at founding.
