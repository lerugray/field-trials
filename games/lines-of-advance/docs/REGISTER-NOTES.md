### 1. REGISTER & TONE ANCHORS

**What it is:** Clinical, operational clarity rendered with the responsiveness of a modern chess GUI. The board is a calculation surface, not a gallery. Palette stays austere (cream/ops-red or stark monochrome with subtle grid lines), UI chrome is minimal, and every interaction is auditable. Movement, contact, and supply resolve with visible, deterministic logic that a player can trace back to the rules. The tone matches the Prussian kriegspiel lineage Ray already transposed to his own site [TD3]: a tool for studying friction, territory, and command, stripped of decorative friction.

**Tone anchors:**
- *Auditable mechanics:* Every roll, line-of-sight check, or engine evaluation is logged and replayable. The player always knows why a result happened.
- *Chassis, not manifesto:* The Situationist theory stays out of the UI. The game presents itself as a functional wargame first, a historical artifact second.
- *Kriegspiel clarity meets chess-engine precision:* Grid lines snap, counters stack cleanly, and the engine runs in the background like a silent referee. The feel is "digital operational table," not "web app."

**What it must NOT be:**
- Situationist cosplay or academic museum framing. No theory quotes, no gallery-style typography, no curatorial voice.
- Clean-infographic SaaS UI or AI-hex-grid defaults [TD1], [TD2]. No dashboard analytics, no floating tooltips that break spatial immersion, no Kickstarter/Ameritrash theme art.
- Decorative animation. Movement is instant or uses a single, functional slide. Combat resolution is a clean table lookup or engine tick, not a cinematic flourish.
- Abstract sea-zone geography or generic wargame tropes [TD1]. Debord's grid is fixed; the UI honors that geometry without trying to "modernize" it into something unrecognizable.

### 2. VARIANT IDEAS

1. **CRT (Combat Results Table) Dice-Odds Variant** — Replaces Debord’s largely deterministic contact resolution with a slim, classical wargame CRT (1:1 to 4:1 columns, 2d6 roll). *Argument:* Injects friction and command uncertainty into the abstract rigidity without bloating the turn structure or breaking the grid’s spatial logic.
2. **CRT (Cathode-Ray Tube) 1981 Display Skin** — Monochrome phosphor rendering, command-line order input, scanline overlay, and deliberate processing ticks that mimic early minimax engines. *Argument:* Honors the "classy modern chess program" pitch by leaning into the actual hardware aesthetic of 1980s computer chess, making the engine’s calculation visible rather than hidden.
3. **Supply-Line Disruption Mode** — Makes Debord’s logistical spine explicit: supply routes render as interactive lines that players can cut, reinforce, or reroute with single-click orders, replacing manual tracking with visual line-of-sight logic. *Argument:* Translates the game’s core attrition mechanic into a frictionless, modern interaction model while preserving the strategic choke-point gameplay.
4. **Fog of War / Referee Engine Hybrid** — One player moves on the main board, the other on a hidden board. A neutral engine resolves contact and reports only what’s visible to each side, logging all hidden movement. *Argument:* Channels the Prussian kriegspiel lineage Ray already cites [S2], turning the abstract grid into a command-and-control exercise without adding dice or tables.
5. **Advisory Evaluation Mode** — The minimax engine runs continuously in the background, offering up to three candidate moves with positional evaluation scores (opt-in, toggleable). *Argument:* Bridges human operational planning with the requested engine, keeping the player in the driver’s seat while exposing the AI’s evaluation logic as a training tool rather than a crutch.

### 3. WORKING TITLES (NEUTRAL, NON-DEBORD)

Ray’s catalog titles run historical, operational, and starkly functional (*The World Undone*, *Imperial Bayonets*, *Shellfire & Fury*, *Violent Schoolmaster Tactical Series*) [S1], [S3]. No indie poetry, no academic framing. Four directions that match that register:

- **Lines of Advance** — Operational, movement-forward, fits the grid-and-supply core without leaning into theory.
- **The War Surface** — Austere, functional, treats the board as a calculation plane rather than a narrative stage.
- **Front & Supply** — Mechanically descriptive, echoes classical SPI/GMT naming conventions [TD2].
- **The Operational Board** — Direct, unpretentious, signals a tool for study and play rather than a museum piece.

### 4. THE RAY-SHAPED ANGLE

A studio port would skin Debord and ship it as an "art game" or a "minimalist wargame app." Ray’s angle is mechanical autopsy and engine verification. He would strip the Situationist mythos, keep the chassis, and rebuild it around three principles that run through his catalog: distill to the quickest playable form while retaining the historical narrative [S3]; define the game by designer intent, not theory [S2]; and use the engine as a design audit, not a gimmick. The minimax isn’t there to beat the player; it’s there to stress-test the board’s balance, expose broken supply loops, and prove the CRT or deterministic resolution holds under optimal play. The UI becomes a digital kriegspiel table where every interaction is logged, auditable, and reproducible. The result isn’t a tribute to Debord’s philosophy—it’s a functional, engine-verified operational tool that uses his grid to teach friction, territory, and command. That’s the Conflict Simulations register: build the machine, verify it, ship it, let the play do the talking.
