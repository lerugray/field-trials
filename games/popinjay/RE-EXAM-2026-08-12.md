## 1 — F3 pixel face — **NOT FIXED, REGRESSED**

The task asked whether the title reads `WALK` / `FIRE WIRE`, and whether body copy with
`M` and `E` reads correctly. It does not.

**What the shipped title card actually renders** (`04-controls-crop-1x.png`,
`01-title-1x.png`, read at native 1×):

| Intended | Renders as |
|---|---|
| `WALK` | `UALK` |
| `FIRE WIRE` | `FIRE UIRE` |
| `CLIMB` | `GLIXB` |
| `UP / DOWN` | `UR / DUUN` |
| `Z OR SPACE` | `2 UR SRAGE` |
| `SIDEARM` | `SIDEARX` |
| `PAUSE` | `RAUSE` |
| `MENUS` | `XENUS` |
| `CONTROLS` | `CUNTRULS` |
| `OPTIONS` | `URFIUNS` |
| `THE TRUNK` | `FHE FRUNK` |
| `EXPOSITION AMUSEMENTS CO.` | `EXRUSIFIUN AXUSEXENTS CU.` |
| `BUILD M7` | `BUILD X7` |
| `PRESS ENTER TO BEGIN THE TOUR` | `RRESS ENTER TU BEUIN THE FUUR` |

Same defect on every other surface: HUD reads `CUXRUSURE` / `UZRE` / `GHAZN` / `SCURE` /
`FZGKEFS`; scorecard reads `BALLUUNS RURRE9`, `SUUVENIRS`, `NEXT UNLUCK`.

Of the four strings the task named, **only `ENTER` reads correctly**. `COMPOSURE`,
`AMUSEMENTS` and `MENUS` are all misrendered. The `E` you asked me to spot is **fine** —
the new `###/#../###/#../##.` reads as a clean E, and the E/5 collision is genuinely
cleared. The `M` is the problem: `#.#/###/.#./###/#.#` has a one-pixel waist in row 3,
making it a **bowtie that reads as X**. `LANE-REPORT-MFIX.md` describes this glyph as
"twin-peak" and left it unchanged while redrawing X; the two are now 7 apart by Hamming
and *both look like X*.

### Objective measure (not eyeballing)

Rendering the shipped `F3` table through its own bitmaps (`02-shipped-f3-alphabet.png`),
and scoring each shipped glyph against the pre-fix face's canonical letterforms by
Hamming nearest-neighbour:

- **18 of 36** alphanumerics are misread or ambiguous — closer to (or tied with) a
  *different* letter's canonical form than their own: `C E F G I K M O P R U W X 0 2 3 8 9`.
  Worst: `P→R` (d=1), `U→X` (d=1), `W→U` (d=1), `0→G` (d=1), `9→8` (d=1), `2→3` (d=1).
- **18 of 36** are clean: `A B D H J L N Q S T V Y Z 1 4 5 6 7`.

Direction of travel (`03-glyphs-before-after.png`, before = `3e97ac3`):

| | Pre-fix face | Shipped face |
|---|---|---|
| Glyph pairs at Hamming ≤1 | **7** (`H/K`, `H/M`, `H/W`, `K/X`, `U/0`, `Z/2`, `0/8`) | 0 |
| Glyph pairs at Hamming ≤2 | — | **0** |
| Glyphs that don't look like their own letter | **0** | **18** |

The audit's original complaint was real — the old face had seven near-identical pairs and
produced `HALK` / `FIRE HIRE`. But every old glyph still *looked like its own letter*. The
fix traded 7 confusable pairs for 18 malformed letterforms. On a build whose pitch is
craft, the first text a player reads is now worse, not better.

### Why 215/215 green did not catch it

`LANE-REPORT-MFIX.md` states it plainly: redrawing X alone left 35 pairs under the new
floor, so **32 further glyphs were adjusted to make the structural test green**. The test
added — *every distinct F3 glyph pair differs by ≥3 lit pixels* — is a **separation**
metric with no **resemblance** term. Optimising 33 glyphs against it produces exactly what
shipped: a face that is maximally distinct and minimally legible. This is the same class of
defect the audit itself flagged at #10 (the ≥95% fill gate that could not fail) — a gate
measuring something other than the thing it claims to measure. Any fix needs a
resemblance gate (render each glyph and check it reads as its own letter) beside the
distance floor, or the same optimisation will recur.

## Register glance

The frame as a whole still reads as one coherent carnival artifact, and the new elements
help rather than hurt: the wind streamers reuse the pennant vocabulary of the bunting, the
amber save strip reads as a period ticket stub, and the badge now sits in clean air. The
5×7 display face (wordmark, `PRIZE COUNTER`, `READY`, the teaching banner) is unaffected
and still excellent.

The single discordant element is the 3×5 body face. It no longer reads as period signage —
it reads as damaged signage, and it is on every panel in the game. The `E` specifically is
good; the `M` is the one to look at.
